Sizing

Pricing

The two numbers that set your price

  1. Volume — the size of the box

    Bigger containers cost more to deliver and haul. But renting too small is the classic false economy: a second delivery plus a second haul fee almost always costs more than sizing up one step the first time.

  2. Weight — the included tonnage

    Every quote should state a weight allowance in tons. Go over it and you pay a per-ton overage fee (often around $55/ton locally). Household junk rarely threatens the limit; shingles, tile, concrete and dirt hit it fast. This is the number to ask about before the truck arrives.

  3. Time — the rental window

    Seven days is the local standard, and it's included in the flat rate. Need longer? Extensions typically run $10–$15 per day — cheap insurance if your contractor's schedule slips.

Five ways Owensboro renters overpay (and how not to)

  • Mixing heavy and bulky debris in one big box. Split them: a small container for the heavy stuff, a bigger one for the bulky stuff.
  • Booking blind on weight. Always get the included tonnage in writing with the quote.
  • Letting rain soak the load. Tarp the container between work sessions — wet debris weighs dramatically more.
  • Blocking the pickup. A truck that can't reach the container gets rescheduled, sometimes with a trip fee. Keep the approach clear on pickup day.
  • Ignoring the fill line. Debris must sit below the rim. Overloaded containers can't legally be hauled and you'll pay for a reload or a swap.

Flat-rate local haulers bundle delivery, pickup, the rental period and disposal into one number. If a quote can't tell you the weight allowance up front, that's your sign to call someone else. Local rules

Owensboro specifics: permits, placement & where it all goes

National dumpster advice skips the part that actually matters — your city, your county, your landfill. Here's the Daviess County version.

🚧 Do you need a permit?

On your driveway or private property: no permit needed. This covers the vast majority of residential rentals.

On a public street or right-of-way: you'll need a right-of-way permit from the City of Owensboro. Downtown lots and tight subdivisions run into this most. Ask your hauler — local companies deal with the city regularly and will tell you straight whether you need one.

♻️ Where your debris actually goes

Owensboro loads typically tip at the Grimes Avenue Transfer Station or the West Daviess County Landfill on KY-815, depending on material type. Disposal fees at these facilities are a real chunk of what you're paying for — it's why weight allowances exist and why overage fees aren't negotiable.

🏠 Driveway protection

A loaded 20-yard container plus the roll-off truck can press 30,000+ pounds onto your concrete. Reputable local haulers lay down wood planks or mats under the container rails as standard practice. If a company doesn't mention driveway protection, ask — it's a five-second question that can save you a cracked driveway.

🏘️ HOAs and tight lots

Several Owensboro subdivisions have HOA rules about how long a container can sit visible from the street. Check your covenants before booking, and if your lot is tight, a 10-yard container's 12-foot length fits where a 22-foot 20-yard won't. Rural properties past Whitesville or Utica rarely have these constraints — but confirm delivery access for the truck on narrow gravel drives.

The estate / whole-house cleanout

Furniture, mattresses, decades of boxes, garage contents — estate cleanouts are high-volume and low-density, which means you run out of space long before you run out of weight. The storm cleanup

Western Kentucky storms — especially the spring cells that roll up the Ohio River — can drop limbs across yards from the riverfront to Horse Fork Creek in a single night. For branches and brush, a 10 or 20-yard works, and keeping yard debris in its own load (not mixed with household junk) usually keeps disposal simpler and cheaper. Cut limbs to manageable lengths and load butt-ends first.

The rules

What can go in — and what absolutely can't

Kentucky waste regulations (and every landfill's scale house) draw a hard line on certain materials. Load a banned item and the whole container can be rejected at the gate — with you paying for the round trip.

✅ Yes, load it

  • Furniture, mattresses, carpet
  • Drywall, lumber, flooring, siding
  • Roofing shingles & underlayment
  • Yard waste, branches, brush
  • Appliances (doors removed from fridges)
  • General household junk & boxes
  • Concrete, brick, dirt (small containers only — mind the weight)

🚫 No — never in the dumpster

  • Hazardous chemicals, solvents, oils
  • Wet paint (dried paint cans are usually fine)
  • Batteries of any kind
  • Tires and railroad ties
  • Asbestos-containing materials
  • Free liquids of any sort
  • Electronics with batteries — check local e-waste options

The rental week

Day by day: how a smooth rental actually runs

2 days before

Book and prep the spot

Get your flat-rate quote with the weight allowance in writing. Clear the placement area — move cars, low branches and basketball hoops. Mark the exact spot with cones or chalk so the driver nails it first try.

Delivery day

The truck arrives (you don't need to be home)

Order before noon and same-day delivery is common around Owensboro. The driver sets protective planks under the rails and positions the container. Walk the placement once it's down — now's the moment to ask for a shift, not on day six.

Days 1–3

Load heavy and flat first

Start with the dense, flat stuff along the bottom and walls — drywall sheets, doors, lumber. This creates a stable base and uses space efficiently. Keep everything below the rim: the fill line is a legal limit, not a suggestion.

Days 4–6

Fill the middle, tarp at night

Toss the irregular bulky items into the center. Throw a tarp over the container each evening — it keeps rain weight out and keeps light debris from blowing into the neighbor's yard during those river-breeze afternoons.

Pickup day

Clear the approach, make the call

Most haulers pick up on call rather than a fixed date — phone it in when you're done. Make sure the truck has a straight, clear path to the container. Once it's gone, do a quick sweep for nails and debris the crew may have missed.
